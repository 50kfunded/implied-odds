\section{Introduction}

Does option-implied variance add forecasting information beyond daily, weekly and monthly realised variance? I compare Corsi's HAR construction, adapted to a direct \HorizonDays{}-day target, with the same regression augmented by the previous completed daily DVOL variance. Raw DVOL and trailing variance provide reference forecasts.

The most striking point-loss ranking is raw DVOL ahead of both fitted regressions. Augmentation reduces mean QLIKE relative to HAR, while its uncertainty interval spans zero and the allocation diagnostic shows a higher point estimate of risk-budget error. The lower loss of raw DVOL highlights estimation risk in the fitted augmentation. The following sections distinguish these estimates from the limits of the evidence and then set out the locked prospective comparison.

\section{Data and method}

The retained inputs contain \RetainedHourlyCandles{} hourly BTC-PERPETUAL candles and \RetainedDVOLCandles{} daily DVOL candles, downloaded on \DownloadDate{}. Evaluation uses daily midnight forecast origins from \EvaluationStartDay{} January \EvaluationStartYear{} through \EvaluationEndDay{} September \EvaluationEndYear{}. Each target is the next \TargetHours{} hourly log-return squares, annualised by \AnnualHours{}; that window requires \RequiredCloses{} completed closes.

Both regressions use exactly the latest \TrainingLabels{} labels whose full \HorizonDays{}-day targets have ended. Prior \DailyDays{}-, \WeeklyDays{}- and \MonthlyDays{}-day annualised variances form the HAR features. The augmented model adds the previous completed daily DVOL close divided by \PercentScale{} and then squared. Level ordinary least squares includes an intercept, with features centred and scaled using training rows only. An annual-variance floor of \VarianceFloorText{} applies to forecasts; raw predictions and every floor hit are retained. Neither regression reaches that floor in this sample.

With $r_t$ denoting an hourly log return and $t$ the forecast origin, the future target and completed-return features are
\begin{align*}
V_t^{(\HorizonDays{}\mathrm{d})} &= \frac{\AnnualHoursPlain{}}{\TargetHours{}}
  \sum_{h=\Unit{}}^{\TargetHours{}} r_{t+h}^{\PowerTwo{}},\\
RV_t^{(k)} &= \frac{\AnnualHoursPlain{}}{\HoursPerDay{}\,k}
  \sum_{h=\Unit{}}^{\HoursPerDay{}\,k}r_{t-h+\Unit{}}^{\PowerTwo{}},
\quad k\in\{\DailyDays{},\WeeklyDays{},\MonthlyDays{}\}.
\end{align*}
The two direct forecasts are
\begin{align*}
\widehat V^{(\HorizonDays{}\mathrm{d}),\mathrm{HAR}}_t
 &= \widehat\beta_{\InterceptIndex{},t}
  +\widehat\beta_{d,t}RV_t^{(\DailyDays{})}
  +\widehat\beta_{w,t}RV_t^{(\WeeklyDays{})}
  +\widehat\beta_{m,t}RV_t^{(\MonthlyDays{})},\\
\widehat V^{(\HorizonDays{}\mathrm{d}),\mathrm{HAR+DVOL}}_t
 &= \widehat\gamma_{\InterceptIndex{},t}
  +\widehat\gamma_{d,t}RV_t^{(\DailyDays{})}
  +\widehat\gamma_{w,t}RV_t^{(\WeeklyDays{})}
  +\widehat\gamma_{m,t}RV_t^{(\MonthlyDays{})}
  +\widehat\gamma_{D,t}
    \left(\frac{\mathrm{DVOL}^{\mathrm{prev.close}}_t}{\PercentScale{}}\right)^{\PowerTwo{}}.
\end{align*}
All comparisons use identical origins. Smaller values of the QLIKE loss indicate more accurate variance forecasts:
\[
\operatorname{QLIKE}
 =\frac{\text{realised}}{\text{forecast}}
  -\log\!\left(\frac{\text{realised}}{\text{forecast}}\right)
  -\QLIKEUnit{}.
\]

\section{Inference limits}

Paired stationary-bootstrap inference uses \BootstrapDraws{} draws, a fixed seed and expected blocks of \ExpectedBlockDays{} days, with a \HACLags{}-lag HAC cross-check. Overlapping targets and rolling fits can create dependence beyond the \HorizonDays{}-day horizon. \section{Limitations}

All historical findings and generated-forecast diagnostics are exploratory. The additional historical work followed inspection of the data, so chronological training alone leaves the evaluation exposed to research choices made after inspection. The interval estimates are descriptive, the Mincer--Zarnowitz and encompassing p-values are diagnostics rather than additional primary discoveries, and the point-loss ranking does not establish formal superiority. ESS is an estimated dependence diagnostic, not an observed count of independent episodes. The non-overlapping phase samples remain dependent; their role is to expose concentration, rather than select a favourable crisis window or identify an ex-ante regime rule. The synthetic stress tests approximate loss dependence rather than the complete rolling HAR estimation pipeline. Zero observed ESS-rule passes leave true pass probability uncertain, as the reported Wilson bound makes explicit.

The original source check compares open, high, low and close (OHLC) fields. It fails because one unused DVOL open differs; all three audited DVOL closes match, but those dates provide limited coverage of the full history. Historical downloads also leave the original unrevised vintages unverified. Returns from one perpetual contract proxy the spot index, and the study covers one asset and venue with overlapping outcomes.

Forecast accuracy and a variance-budget diagnostic define the economic scope. The latter assumes constant fractional exposure; a fixed-unit portfolio, rebalancing, funding and profit lie outside the implementation. The allocation rule has no established optimality for squared budget error. Fee amounts remain separate from that error, so the results identify neither net trading returns, a causal variance premium nor an executable edge. The cumulative figure measures loss accumulation. The separate option-distribution study retains finite calendar-grid and extrapolated-tail limitations.

\section{Prospective protocol}

The primary prospective contrast remains HAR + DVOL minus HAR. An inference-validation amendment disables formal superiority claims for the planned year, which collects timestamped evidence under the fixed design. A later inference design needs a separate prospective declaration; the current cohort and terminal date stay fixed even if the result is inconclusive.

The HAR cohort contains \PlannedOrigins{} scheduled origins from \HARStartDate{} through \HAREndDate{}, each at \HAROriginTime{} UTC. Features end at midnight, forecasts freeze between \HARFreezeStartTime{} and \HARFreezeEndTime{}, and outcomes begin at \HAROriginTime{}. Training uses the correspondingly shifted historical \HorizonDays{}-day targets completed by midnight, a different alignment from the retrospective midnight targets. Final maturity is \HARMaturityDate{}, with terminal analysis fixed at \HARAnalysisDate{}, \HAROriginTime{} UTC.

Each new forecast requires matching hourly and daily DVOL closes and completion of fitting and the durable write before the freeze deadline. Missed scheduled days count as missed and stay unfilled. Terminal intervals require all \PlannedOrigins{} planned pairs and source checks; the fixed analysis cutoff excludes later-arriving scores.

A separate option-distribution study uses stochastic volatility inspired (SVI) option smiles to define probability forecasts for later settlement outcomes. It has zero settled research forecasts. Its original protocol and baseline locks remain unchanged. A versioned amendment sets the expiry cutoff at \SVICutoffDate{}, \SVITime{} UTC and the final decision at \SVIAnalysisDate{}, \SVITime{} UTC. Interim reporting and command-line inference remain descriptive, with terminal data availability fixed.

\section{Infrastructure and reproduction}

Immutable raw capture segments, code hashes and input hashes accompany the forecasts. Recoverable delivery-pagination failures trigger retries in fresh segments; conflicting immutable prices remain fatal. Cleaning is cached by input, protocol, code and runtime fingerprints.
