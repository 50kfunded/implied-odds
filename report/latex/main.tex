\begin{center}{\LARGE\bfseries Does DVOL add information beyond HAR?\par}\end{center}
\begin{center}\small A retrospective BTC variance forecast study | \PaperAuthor{} | \PaperDate{}\end{center}

\textbf{Abstract.} Does option-implied variance improve Bitcoin's direct \HorizonDays{}-day variance forecasts beyond a heterogeneous autoregressive (HAR) benchmark? I first compare HAR, HAR plus Deribit's option-implied volatility index (DVOL) and raw DVOL on \MatchedOrigins{} historical origins, then evaluate a declared set of \VTwoModels{} specifications on a separate \VTwoN{}-origin common cohort. Longer estimation windows lower HAR + DVOL's mean quasi-likelihood (QLIKE) loss. Log-HAR plus DVOL with training-only smearing has the lowest point loss in the broader comparison, while the focal QLIKE-fitted HAR + DVOL versus QLIKE-fitted HAR bootstrap interval includes zero. The data do not separate estimation noise, model misspecification and changes in forecast scale. Inference remains a limitation: the declared subsampling alternative fails the synthetic size checks, and the model confidence set does not establish a winner. The next step is a separately declared prospective comparison of matched positive-variance models; the existing year remains best-effort evidence collection without a formal significance test.

\section{Introduction}

Does option-implied variance add forecasting information beyond daily, weekly and monthly realised variance? I compare a direct \HorizonDays{}-day HAR forecast with HAR + DVOL, which adds the previous completed daily DVOL variance. Raw DVOL provides a reference forecast.

\begin{samepage}
Raw DVOL's original paired interval against HAR excludes zero, but its effective sample size (ESS) is below the reporting rule and I highlighted it after inspection. Rolling rescaling and the equal blend leave the ranking uncertain.
\end{samepage}

Prior work asks whether option-implied volatility adds information beyond historical volatility. \citet{christensenprabhala} find incremental information in implied volatility for the S\&P \SPIndex{} index using non-overlapping observations; their setting differs from the overlapping BTC targets here. \citet{corsi} combines realised-volatility components across time horizons in the HAR model, which I adapt to a direct variance target. \citet{patton} studies how imperfect volatility proxies affect forecast rankings and motivates care in choosing the loss function.

Using Deribit option trades, \citet{hoangbaur} compare implied volatility with time-series forecasts, including HAR. They find horizon-dependent accuracy and gains from combining implied volatility with model forecasts. Their option-derived measure and shorter forecast horizons differ from the direct \HorizonDays{}-day DVOL comparison here.

\section{Data and method}

The retained inputs contain \RetainedHourlyCandles{} hourly BTC-PERPETUAL candles and \RetainedDVOLCandles{} daily DVOL candles, downloaded on \DownloadDate{}. Evaluation uses daily midnight forecast origins from \EvaluationStartDay{} January \EvaluationStartYear{} through \EvaluationEndDay{} September \EvaluationEndYear{}. Each target is the next \TargetHours{} hourly log-return squares, annualised by \AnnualHours{}; that window requires \RequiredCloses{} completed closes.

Both regressions use exactly the latest \TrainingLabels{} labels whose full \HorizonDays{}-day targets have ended. Prior \DailyDays{}-, \WeeklyDays{}- and \MonthlyDays{}-day annualised variances form the HAR features. HAR + DVOL adds the previous completed daily DVOL close divided by \PercentScale{} and then squared. Level ordinary least squares includes an intercept, with features centred and scaled using training rows only. An annual-variance floor of \VarianceFloorText{} applies to forecasts; raw predictions and every floor hit are retained. Neither regression reaches that floor in this sample. Level OLS minimises squared variance errors, while evaluation uses QLIKE. These are different objectives within the restricted HAR specifications, although both losses target conditional mean variance when forecasts are unrestricted \citep{patton}.

With $r_t$ denoting an hourly log return and $t$ the forecast origin, the future target and completed-return features are
\begin{align*}
V_t^{(\HorizonDays{}\mathrm{d})} &= \frac{\AnnualHoursPlain{}}{\TargetHours{}}
  \sum_{h=\Unit{}}^{\TargetHours{}} r_{t+h}^{\PowerTwo{}},\\
RV_t^{(k)} &= \frac{\AnnualHoursPlain{}}{\HoursPerDay{}\,k}
  \sum_{h=\Unit{}}^{\HoursPerDay{}\,k}r_{t-h+\Unit{}}^{\PowerTwo{}},
\quad k\in\{\DailyDays{},\WeeklyDays{},\MonthlyDays{}\}.
\end{align*}
The two direct forecasts are
\begin{align*}
\widehat V^{(\HorizonDays{}\mathrm{d}),\mathrm{HAR}}_t
 &= \widehat\beta_{\InterceptIndex{},t}
  +\widehat\beta_{d,t}RV_t^{(\DailyDays{})}
  +\widehat\beta_{w,t}RV_t^{(\WeeklyDays{})}
  +\widehat\beta_{m,t}RV_t^{(\MonthlyDays{})},\\
\widehat V^{(\HorizonDays{}\mathrm{d}),\mathrm{HAR+DVOL}}_t
 &= \widehat\gamma_{\InterceptIndex{},t}
  +\widehat\gamma_{d,t}RV_t^{(\DailyDays{})}
  +\widehat\gamma_{w,t}RV_t^{(\WeeklyDays{})}
  +\widehat\gamma_{m,t}RV_t^{(\MonthlyDays{})}
  +\widehat\gamma_{D,t}
    \left(\frac{\mathrm{DVOL}^{\mathrm{prev.close}}_t}{\PercentScale{}}\right)^{\PowerTwo{}}.
\end{align*}
All comparisons use identical origins. Smaller values of the QLIKE loss indicate more accurate variance forecasts:
\[
\operatorname{QLIKE}
 =\frac{\text{realised}}{\text{forecast}}
  -\log\!\left(\frac{\text{realised}}{\text{forecast}}\right)
  -\QLIKEUnit{}.
\]

\section{Testing scale and fixed-weight alternatives}

The original HAR + DVOL minus HAR mean QLIKE difference is \PairedLossMean{}, with descriptive interval [\PairedLossLower{}, \PairedLossUpper{}] and ESS \PairedLossEffectiveN{}. Raw DVOL minus (HAR + DVOL) has mean \RawPairedMean{}, interval [\RawPairedLower{}, \RawPairedUpper{}] and ESS \RawPairedESS{}.

I declared the added specifications in public commit \texttt{\ExtensionSpecificationCommit{}} before evaluating these additions, after inspecting the original ranking. Rescaled DVOL uses exactly the same latest \TrainingLabels{} fully completed labels as each HAR fit. With $D_t$ denoting raw DVOL variance, $V_s$ the realised target and $\mathcal T_t$ that training set, the added forecasts are
\[
c_t=\frac{\Unit{}}{\TrainingLabels{}}\sum_{s\in\mathcal T_t}\frac{V_s}{D_s},\qquad
\widehat V_t^{\mathrm{rescaled}}=c_tD_t,\qquad
\widehat V_t^{\mathrm{equal}}=\BlendWeight{}\widehat V_t^{\mathrm{HAR}}+\BlendWeight{}D_t.
\]
The multiplier is positive and unclipped. The blend averages variances and has no fitted combination weight; HAR's coefficients remain estimated. All rows use the same origins, bootstrap draws and ESS machinery, while the locked prospective primary comparison stays unchanged.

Raw DVOL minus HAR has mean \RawHarMean{}, descriptive \ConfidencePercent{}\% interval [\RawHarLower{}, \RawHarUpper{}] and ESS \RawHarESS{}. The original pipeline already computed this contrast, but I chose to highlight it after seeing the ranking, so this is a post-inspection highlight rather than a pre-declared test. I therefore treat it as descriptive.

QLIKE penalises a forecast too low by a factor $k>\Unit{}$ more than one too high by the same factor. I therefore check scale. Mean realised-to-forecast variance ratios are \MeanVarianceRatioTrailing{} for trailing variance over the previous \MonthlyDays{} days, \MeanVarianceRatioDvol{} for raw DVOL, \MeanVarianceRatioHar{} for HAR and \MeanVarianceRatioHardvol{} for HAR + DVOL. Each averages individual $V_t/\widehat V_t$ ratios, rather than dividing sample means. A single scalar equal to that mean minimises in-sample QLIKE. It lowers raw DVOL's in-sample mean from \MeanQLIKEDvol{} to \InSampleDvolQLIKE{} and HAR + DVOL's to \InSampleAugmentedQLIKE{}.

The completed-label rolling rule reaches \MeanQLIKERescaled{}, so most of the full-sample scaling gain is absent from the rolling forecast. This gap is consistent with changes in optimal scale or rolling estimation noise; it does not identify scale drift. Even after full-sample rescaling, raw DVOL's mean loss of \InSampleDvolQLIKE{} remains below unscaled HAR + DVOL's \MeanQLIKEHardvol{}. These in-sample diagnostics lean against helpful constant upward scaling as the explanation for raw DVOL's advantage; they establish neither conditional calibration nor a causal explanation.

\subsection{Extreme differences pull against the mean}

The largest \TailOnePercent{}\% and \TailFivePercent{}\% of absolute HAR + DVOL minus HAR QLIKE differences account for \TailOneSharePercent{}\% and \TailFiveSharePercent{}\% of total absolute differences. Their aggregate signed contributions are positive, so these groups hurt HAR + DVOL relative to HAR even though the full-sample mean is negative. This pattern runs against a simple explanation in which HAR + DVOL wins by avoiding the most extreme losses.

\subsection{The ranking depends on the specification}

After inspecting v1 and the review, I publicly declared v2 in commit \texttt{\VTwoCommit{}} and amended its focal contrast in \texttt{\VTwoAmendment{}} before evaluation. A strict window check uses \VTwoWindowN{} common origins; the broader \VTwoModels{}-model comparison uses a later \VTwoN{}-origin cohort so every model has sufficient completed training labels. Its means are not directly comparable with Table~\ref{tab:forecasts}.

The additions include log-HAR, positive log-link QLIKE fits with and without ridge shrinkage, longer and expanding windows, EWMA, GARCH, EGARCH, a constrained Bates--Granger-style combination, and Mincer--Zarnowitz-corrected DVOL. Log and QLIKE fits use \VTwoLabels{} completed labels. Training-only residual smearing back-transforms log fits \citep{duan}; it does not establish conditional unbiasedness. Combination weights use completed reconstructed past-only HAR forecasts, not fitted values or original unrevised vintages. The \href{https://github.com/50kfunded/implied-odds/blob/main/protocol/exploratory-review-v2.json}{declaration} fixes all settings and failure rules.

Log-HAR + DVOL has mean QLIKE \VTwoLogAug{}, versus \VTwoLog{} for log-HAR and \VTwoRaw{} for raw DVOL. QLIKE-fitted HAR + DVOL scores \VTwoQAug{} and QLIKE-fitted HAR scores \VTwoQHar{}: their focal difference is \VTwoFocal{}, bootstrap interval [\VTwoLower{}, \VTwoUpper{}], ESS \VTwoESS{}. Longer-window results and all model rows appear in the \href{https://github.com/50kfunded/implied-odds/blob/main/report/v2-results.md}{complete results}. These exploratory changes show sensitivity to specification; they do not identify estimation noise as the cause of the original ranking.

\section{Inference limits}
\label{sec:inference}

Paired stationary-bootstrap inference uses \BootstrapDraws{} draws, a fixed seed and expected blocks of \ExpectedBlockDays{} days, with a \HACLags{}-lag heteroskedasticity- and autocorrelation-consistent (HAC) cross-check. Block sensitivities use \SensitivityThirtyDays{}, \SensitivityNinetyDays{}, \SensitivityOneEightyDays{} and \SensitivityYearDays{} days. Overlapping targets and rolling fits can create dependence beyond the \HorizonDays{}-day horizon. For each paired QLIKE difference series, ESS is the minimum of the observation count, an estimate from positive autocorrelation pairs constrained to decrease, and a variance-ratio estimate using the \HACLags{}-lag Newey--West long-run variance; the pair sequence stops at its first nonpositive pair. The existing plan fixes \MinimumEffectiveN{} as a minimum reporting rule to withhold confirmatory results when estimated information is limited; I retain it without tuning it to these results, but the study provides no size or power derivation that makes this cutoff sufficient for valid inference.

Each declared synthetic scenario has \SimulationReplications{} independent replications at sample sizes \SimulationShortN{} and \SimulationLongNGrouped{}, with \SimulationBootstrapDraws{} bootstrap draws per replication. The population null has exactly zero mean, without centring each realised sample. The scenarios use a \HorizonDays{}-day moving-average loss component alone, add a stationary slow component of persistence $\exp(\NegativeUnit{}/\PersistenceDays{})$, or add symmetric rare shocks spread across overlapping windows. The alternative shifts the mean by \AlternativeShift{} marginal standard deviations, rather than \AlternativeShift{} QLIKE.

They describe assumptions fitted to an inspected loss series, not the power of a future market sample. The exploratory model confidence set \citep{mcs} retains \VTwoMCSSize{} models; inclusion does not establish equal accuracy.

\section{Limitations}

\paragraph{Research choices.} I added the historical comparisons, including v2, after inspecting the data. V2 was declared before its evaluation on that same inspected history. Chronological training does not remove that exposure to research choices. All historical findings and generated-forecast diagnostics are exploratory; the intervals are descriptive, and additional diagnostics are not primary discoveries. The mean QLIKE ranking establishes no formal superiority. I chose to report the raw DVOL minus HAR comparison after inspection; the raw DVOL minus (HAR + DVOL), rescaling and fixed-blend comparisons also follow inspection. A one-parameter rolling fit still has estimation risk because its overlapping labels are dependent. The added intervals do not turn these mechanism checks into confirmatory tests.

\paragraph{Data.} The original source check compares open, high, low and close (OHLC) fields. It fails because one unused DVOL open differs. All three audited DVOL closes match, but those dates cover little of the full history. I cannot verify the original unrevised data vintages from these historical downloads. One perpetual contract supplies a proxy for spot-index returns, so the study covers one asset and venue.

\paragraph{Scope.} I evaluate variance forecasts and a variance-budget diagnostic. The diagnostic assumes constant fractional exposure; it does not model a fixed-unit portfolio, rebalancing, funding or profit. I have not established that the rule minimises squared variance-budget error. I make no claims about net trading returns, a causal variance premium or an executable edge. The cumulative figure tracks QLIKE differences, rather than portfolio returns.

\paragraph{Inference.} The non-overlapping phase samples can still be dependent because volatility and rolling parameter estimates persist. All starting phases reuse the same history; favourable counts are a dependent stability display, with no binomial interpretation or ex-ante regime rule. ESS estimates dependence-adjusted information rather than counting independent episodes. The synthetic stress tests approximate loss dependence, not the full rolling HAR estimation pipeline. None of the declared one-year replications passes the ESS rule, but zero observed passes does not mean the true probability is zero; the Wilson bound quantifies that uncertainty.

\section{Prospective protocol}

The primary prospective contrast remains HAR + DVOL minus HAR. Under the inference-validation amendment, I treat the planned year as timestamped evidence collection without a formal significance test. I fix the cohort and terminal date in advance, even if the result is inconclusive; a later inference design needs a separate prospective declaration.

The HAR cohort contains \PlannedOrigins{} scheduled origins from \HARStartDate{} through \HAREndDate{}, each at \HAROriginTime{} UTC. Features end at midnight, forecasts freeze between \HARFreezeStartTime{} and \HARFreezeEndTime{}, and outcomes begin at \HAROriginTime{}. Training uses the correspondingly shifted historical \HorizonDays{}-day targets completed by midnight, a different alignment from the retrospective midnight targets. Final maturity is \HARMaturityDate{}, with terminal analysis fixed at \HARAnalysisDate{}, \HAROriginTime{} UTC.

Each new forecast requires matching hourly and daily DVOL closes and completion of fitting and the durable write before the freeze deadline. Missed scheduled days count as missed and stay unfilled. Terminal intervals require all \PlannedOrigins{} planned pairs and source checks; the fixed analysis cutoff excludes later-arriving scores.
